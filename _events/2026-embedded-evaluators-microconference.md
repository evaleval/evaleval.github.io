## 📖 Background

Embedded evaluators are commonplace in public health[^1], education[^2], banking, and nuclear energy, and interest in applying this approach to AI evaluation is growing. The AI Evaluator Forum's open letter calling for the [Minimum Conditions for Embedding Evaluators](https://aievaluatorforum.org/initiatives/embedded-evaluation-letter) has drawn more than 100 signatories, while Anthropic[^3] and OpenAI[^4] have committed to giving external evaluators sustained, employee-level access to internal systems. These developments raise a natural question. What should embedded AI evaluation look like in practice?

This microconference brings together third-party evaluators, model developers, policymakers, and civil society organizations to examine the conditions that make embedded AI evaluation independent, rigorous, and consequential. We look at which entities should host embedded evaluators, what access they need to internal systems such as training data, model weights, and APIs, and how that access is sustained. We also examine governance, including how evaluators are funded, selected, and validated, who sets their scope, and what protects their ability to report unfavorable findings while safeguarding sensitive information. Finally, we ask how findings translate into action and who is responsible for follow-through.

## 🎯 Goals

- **Clarify scope and assumptions.** Identify which domains, systems, risks, and decisions embedded evaluations address across model, application, and downstream dependency layers, and where their limitations lie.
- **Identify conditions for independence and rigor.** Examine evaluator selection, funding, sustained access, methods, transparency, and protections for evaluators and employees.
- **Connect findings to action.** Explore how findings inform deployment decisions, incident response, and public accountability, including responsibility for follow-through.
- **Map agreement and unresolved tensions.** Examine differences across stakeholder groups, how embedded evaluation could favor larger, better-resourced organizations, and how non-embedded findings retain salience.
- **Define collaborative next steps.** Identify research questions, practical experiments, and implementation priorities, including a possible pilot embedded evaluation effort.
